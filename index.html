The idea in two sentences

Your view on each measure's goal sets the direction, so if you are against the goal it leans NO, full stop. If you are for the goal, the measure's build quality decides how firm that YES is, so a good idea with a weak plan shows up as a Close call.

How your lean is worked out

  1. How well-built. For each measure, the tool averages the guide's seven ratings, counting each one as much as your slider says. A factor set to Ignore does not count at all. The result runs from 1 (weak) to 5 (strong).
  2. Your answer. Against or Strongly against always gives a NO lean, because good design cannot fix a goal you do not want. Not sure gives no lean.
  3. For answers. When you are for the goal, the build score sets how firm the YES is, using the table below.

What a Close call means. You like the goal, but the measure scores weak on the things you said matter. Open its facts and read the biggest risk and unknown. Some people vote yes anyway because the goal matters more than the details. Others vote no and wait for a better-written version.

Behind the table, the tool scales How well-built to a 0 to 1 range, (score − 1) ÷ 4, multiplies it by your answer (For = 1, Strongly for = 2), and compares the result with 1.2 and 0.5. Ratings come from the propositions guide's scorecard and are analytical judgments based on the Legislative Analyst's Office and independent sources. This tool sorts your own priorities and does not recommend a vote.
